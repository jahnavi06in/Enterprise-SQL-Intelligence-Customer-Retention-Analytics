# Business Recommendations and Next Steps

## Purpose

This section translates the descriptive findings from the historical Online Retail dataset into questions a business team could investigate. These are recommendations for further analysis—not claims that the dataset proves a particular action will increase revenue.

## 1. Investigate the UK's large share of recorded revenue

The United Kingdom accounts for most of the recorded revenue in this dataset.

**Suggested next step:** Break UK sales down by month, product, and customer segment. Check whether the concentration reflects the underlying business footprint, customer mix, or a small number of unusually large orders.

**Decision this could support:** Where to focus inventory planning and sales reporting, after confirming the company's current operating markets.

## 2. Review high-revenue products alongside margin and returns

The product revenue chart identifies items with high recorded sales.

**Suggested next step:** Join product-level costs, discounts, stock-outs, and returns data. Compare revenue with gross margin and order frequency before deciding which products deserve more attention.

**Decision this could support:** Product assortment and inventory prioritisation. Revenue alone is not enough to recommend increasing stock.

## 3. Understand repeat purchasing by cohort

The cohort chart shows how repeat purchasing changes after a customer's first recorded purchase.

**Suggested next step:** Compare cohorts using the same follow-up window and check whether particular months have incomplete observation periods. If marketing campaign or acquisition-channel data is available, compare retention by channel.

**Decision this could support:** Which customer groups or acquisition channels merit closer retention analysis.

## 4. Treat one-time buyers as a research question

Some identified customers have only one recorded invoice during the observed period.

**Suggested next step:** Check whether they purchased near the end of the dataset, whether the period is long enough to observe a second purchase, and whether product category or order size differs from repeat buyers.

**Decision this could support:** Designing a measured follow-up or customer research test. The current dataset alone does not show why a customer did not return.

## 5. Use RFM groups as a starting point, not a final targeting rule

RFM segmentation summarises recency, purchase frequency, and recorded spend.

**Suggested next step:** Review the thresholds with business stakeholders, check how sensitive segment sizes are to those thresholds, and validate segments on a later time period if available.

**Decision this could support:** Prioritising further customer analysis. Any marketing action should be tested and evaluated rather than assumed to work.

## 6. Add measures that are missing from transaction data

The current analysis is mainly about sales transactions. It does not include all the information needed to assess business health.

Useful additions would include:
- Product cost and gross margin.
- Refund and return details.
- Marketing spend and acquisition channel.
- Customer support contacts or satisfaction measures.
- Inventory availability and delivery performance.
- More recent transaction data.

## Limitations to keep in mind

- The dataset covers December 2010 to December 2011 and should not be treated as current market evidence.
- Recorded revenue is not profit.
- Observed associations do not establish causation.
- Recommendations should be revisited after checking definitions, data quality, and the business context.

## Analyst's takeaway

The goal of the analysis is not to prescribe a business action from one chart. It is to surface patterns, explain what they may mean, identify what is still unknown, and define the next analysis that would help a team make a better-informed decision.
